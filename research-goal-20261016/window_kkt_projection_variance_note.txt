KKT-aware signed projection and a compressed derivative-variance lift
2026-10-17

Scope
-----
The identities and equivalence statements below are finite-dimensional
algebra. Their application to the actual kernel needs the stated actual
KKT and scalar Taylor-slab hypotheses. This note proves no whole-domain
cover or zero proportion. The companion bounded pilot uses only the first
of the already frozen six remaining cells, its unchanged midpoint-energy
packet and target, at most two rounds of sixteen cuts, and a 45-second
limit per HiGHS-IPM solve. No Lean or full-cover job is launched here.

1. What KKT can and cannot say about the signed projection loss
------------------------------------------------------------
Write F(g)=P(g)+E(g), P=b.g, E=<k,k>_a, where a_e>0 and k_e is the actual
amplitude at the actual span. Let J be the 41-by-9 Jacobian of the amplitude
k, not the vector of w'=2*k*k' used in the graph LP. At an orthant minimum,
  b+2*J^T*W*k=lambda, lambda>=0,
  lambda_r*(g_r-sep)=0.
Consequently, for any direction d supported on strictly free coordinates,
  <J*d,k>_a=-b.d/2.                                      (1)
These are two-sided free directions. At a boundary a merely one-sided
feasible direction only gives a variational inequality; it does not give
(1). In general the right side is (lambda.d-b.d)/2.

The signed comparison vector is arbitrary. If it is chosen as u=J*d,
then its discarded component is exactly
  <u,k>_a^2/<u,u>_a=(b.d)^2/[4*<J*d,J*d>_a],             (2)
provided the denominator is positive. A pressure-neutral free direction
b.d=0 therefore gives zero discarded component: the signed projection is
the exact actual E at this minimum. For two free coordinates i,j one may
choose d=b_j*e_i-b_i*e_j, without any inverse or numerical rank claim.
If J*d=0, the separate Q=0 branch is exact as well.

The radial excess h=g-sep*1 is also supported on free coordinates. Hence
  <J*h,k>_a=-(P-sep*sum b)/2.                             (3)
If h!=0 and every b_r>0, the pressure excess is positive and J*h cannot
vanish. This gives a pressure-related loss identity for the comparison
vector J*h. A cell-wide upper bound qbar on ||J*h||_a^2 gives the valid
KKT energy bound E>=(P-sep*sum b)^2/(4*qbar). Such a payment may be small;
no useful global numerical bound is asserted here.

For the original phase comparison u0=A*sin(pi*span), choose any free d
and put r=u0-J*d. Then at a KKT point,
  (<u0,k>_a+b.d/2)^2 <= ||r||_a^2*E.                    (4)
Under F<c this yields
  |<u0,k>_a| >= max(|b.d|/2-sqrt(||r||_a^2*(c-P)),0).
Its square divided by Q=||u0||_a^2 is a valid extra lower payment for the
discarded term when Q>0. Positive payment requires a proved closeness
estimate relating the actual phase comparison to an amplitude-Jacobian
direction. KKT alone does not provide that estimate.

An exact obstruction to any general pressure-only claim is the smooth
coercive one-coordinate model
  x>=0, b=2, k(x)=(x-2,1), F(x)=2*x+(x-2)^2+1.
Its unique minimum is x=1, where P=2, k=(-1,1), J=(1,0) and
  b+2*J^T*k=0.
For comparison u=(1,1), Q=2 and <u,k>=0, so the discarded component is
zero. For comparison u=k it is E=2. Thus the same pressure and exact KKT
data permit either extreme when no relation between u and J is supplied.
This is a counterexample to a general algebraic inference, not to the
actual zeta kernel or its proposed floor. Likewise, choosing u so that
the projection is exact is not by itself a new bound: u=0 is already exact
at every point. One still needs a simpler uniform payment for the new
residual, or additional cuts which retain information discarded before.

2. The proposed derivative-moment extension
-----------------------------------------
Use centred/scaled gap variables y in [-1,1]^9 and x=(1,y), of length 10.
Here u_e denotes the actual scalar derivative w'(span_e), not the signed
projection comparison from section 1. The existing 464 variables retain
  means of g and u,
  X=<x*x^T>, encoded by the 45 symmetric y-y products,
  z_e=<x*u_e>, encoded by the 369 y-u products and the means of u.
The moment brackets are a linear pseudo-expectation. An actual point has
rank-one moments, but a relaxation must not impose that as an assumption.

The old scalar oracle supplies an actual affine slab
  |u_e-ell_e(y)|<=eps_e, ell_e(y)=beta_e^T*x, eps_e>=0.    (5)
All beta and eps are fixed rational data for the current cell. For the
actual old.build tuple (P,m0,m1,err,kind), its exact scale is
  ell_e(g)=m0/DS+(m1/DS)*(span_e(g)-P/(2*SC)),
  eps_e=err/DS.
P is a DOUBLED centre coordinate. In y coordinates,
  beta_0=m0/DS+(m1/DS)*(span_e(center)-P/(2*SC)),
  beta_r=(m1/DS)*radius_r for r in the span, zero otherwise.
The pilot checks both signed slab rows against old.build's original
integer coefficient and RHS data, with Q=2*SC*DS*D.

A direct large lift would introduce U=<u*u^T> (861 symmetric entries),
require
  M=[[X,Z],[Z^T,U]] >=PSD 0,
and add 41 scalar quadratic-slab rows
  U_ee-2*beta_e^T*z_e+beta_e^T*X*beta_e <= eps_e^2.        (6)
For a free KKT equality H_r=c_r^T*x+a_r^T*u=0, the existing first and
y-multiplied rows say
  X*c_r+Z*a_r=0.                                         (7)
Multiplication by all u_e would additionally demand
  Z^T*c_r+U*a_r=0.                                       (8)
Only proven free KKT equalities can be used this way. Multiplication of a
one-sided KKT inequality by a derivative of unknown sign is invalid.

3. Exact compression to 41 eleven-dimensional PSD blocks
-------------------------------------------------------
Under the precise constraint policy stated below, all of the large lift's
information on the old 464 variables is equivalent to requiring, for
each e,
  N_e=[[X, z_e-X*beta_e],
       [(z_e-X*beta_e)^T, eps_e^2]] >=PSD 0.              (9)
There is no U variable in (9). The matrices have size 11, not 51.

Forward implication: from a large M>=0 form the Gram vector for the
residual u_e-beta_e^T*x. Its norm squared is the left side of (6). Its
cross products with the x vectors are z_e-X*beta_e. Replacing its diagonal
by the larger eps_e^2 preserves PSD, proving (9).

Reverse implication, including singular X: choose Gram vectors X_i for
X. PSD of N_e implies r_e=z_e-X*beta_e lies in range(X) and
  r_e^T*X^dagger*r_e <= eps_e^2.
Choose a vector R_e in span{X_i} with <X_i,R_e>=(r_e)_i and that minimum
norm; set U_e=R_e+sum_i(beta_e)_i*X_i. All old z_e are preserved, the joint
Gram matrix of the X_i and U_e is PSD, and (6) holds. No invertibility of
X is required. This is a Gram-space existence argument, not an instruction
to numerically invert a nearly singular moment matrix.

Furthermore (7) says the Gram vector
  sum_i(c_r)_i*X_i+sum_e(a_r)_e*U_e
is perpendicular to every X_i. It also belongs to their span, so it is
zero. Therefore (8) holds automatically for this extension.

Exact equivalence policy: all remaining constraints and the objective
depend only on the old 464 variables. The only uses of U are joint PSD,
the upper slab norms (6), and the free KKT products (8). Under this policy,
the u-multiplied KKT rows add no further old-variable projection strength.
One may equivalently project every U_e onto span{X_i}; this preserves old
moments, decreases every residual norm, and kills the free KKT vector.

This equivalence need not survive added u-u cross-RLT constraints,
additional nonmonotone U rows, fixed U cross moments, or new lower bounds
on residual variance. Those could make a 1325-variable model stronger.
They are not part of the compressed pilot. The claim also does not say a
finite set of cuts enforces exact PSD: it is only an outer approximation.

4. Exact rational cuts and a strictness example
---------------------------------------------
For any rational v in R^10 and t in R, (9) gives the linear moment row
  v^T*X*v+2*t*v^T*(z_e-X*beta_e)+t^2*eps_e^2 >=0.         (10)
Its actual-point factorization is exactly
  (v^T*x+t*delta_e)^2+t^2*(eps_e^2-delta_e^2) >=0,
  delta_e=u_e-beta_e^T*x.
Both terms are nonnegative by (5). This uses only the already proved
actual scalar slab; no new transcendental table or unknown matrix rank.
A negative numerical eigenvector merely selects v,t. After quantizing
them to integers, every coefficient is regenerated with Fraction and the
factorization is expanded independently before a row is accepted.

These rows genuinely strengthen position PSD, boxes, and KKT*y. Consider
the exact toy moments
  <y>=<u1>=<u2>=0, <y^2>=1/4,
  <y*u1>=1/5, <y*u2>=-1/5,
with y in [-1,1], u_i in [-3/10,3/10], ell_i=0, eps_i=3/10, and free KKT
equation H=u1+u2=0. Position PSD holds. All four box McCormick rows for
y*u_i hold because |1/5|<=3/10. H's first moment and y product are zero.
But N_1 with v=(0,1), t=-2 gives
  1/4-4/5+4*(3/10)^2=-19/100<0.
Thus no proposed extended PSD/slab model has these old moments, while
the old constraints allow them. This is a strict projected strengthening,
not merely exclusion of an arbitrarily chosen U completion.

5. Bounded-pilot interpretation
------------------------------
cover_residual_psd_pilot.py uses only the first row of the immutable
cover-cross-moment-remaining-six.json. It keeps its midpoint energy packet
and all old derivative graph, KKT, cross-moment RLT and McCormick rows.
Each LP is HiGHS-IPM with time_limit=45, maximum two cut rounds, and no
more than sixteen quantized cuts per round. It saves a durable receipt
before/after solves and after each cut batch. Exact nonnegative Farkas
extraction is attempted only if an energy-slack solve gives a positive
hint. A floating hint or a negative eigenvalue is never a cell exclusion.

Required eventual proof inputs are actual Taylor slabs on the current
cell, exact centred-variable identities, actual free-coordinate KKT,
sound energy packets, and exact nonnegative aggregation/box rounding.
Even a successful local pilot would not prove a complete minimizer cover,
an unconditional zero proportion, or a submission-ready source.

The bounded residual pilot completed three IPMs (initial plus two batches)
in about 1.34, 1.42, and 1.37 seconds. All energy slacks were zero; the final
residual matrices still have negative eigenvalue hints. Thirty-two exact
cuts passed a separate stdlib-only arithmetic replay. No exact feasible
PSD primal or exclusion is asserted; no extra rounds were run.

6. Reverse Taylor and the stronger fixed-h Bregman energy row
------------------------------------------------------------
For a scalar f on an interval containing s0 and s, f''<=M implies
  f(s)>=f(s0)+f'(s)*(s-s0)-M*(s-s0)^2/2.                 (11)
This is the ordinary upper Taylor estimate expanded from s back to s0.
It differs from the old energy packet, which uses f'(s0) and f'' LOWER.
The old scalar graph builder has derivative slabs and KKT rows, not (11).

Using the existing actual derivative variables u_e=f'(s_e), the sum gives
  F(g)>=F0_lower+P(g)-P(g0)
        +sum_e a_e*[u_e*(s_e-s0_e)-M_e*(s_e-s0_e)^2/2]. (12)
All terms fit the old 464 variables: g, u, y-u, and y-y. The scalar upper
M_e is the upper half of the same whole-span SecondOracle query whose
lower half was already used by the midpoint packet. No new anchor point
is needed. At all-free KKT, the pressure difference and mixed derivative
term cancel to (g-g0).grad F(g)=0. Thus (12) is then equivalent to
  F(g)>=F0_lower-sum_e a_e*M_e*(s_e-s0_e)^2/2.
At a boundary one cannot drop the gradient term without a valid sign or
support argument.

The single reverse pilot kept the old energy packet and added only (12).
One 45-second-limited IPM took about 1.37 seconds and returned zero common
energy slack. This does not exclude the fixed first remaining cell.

Now assume, on the scalar segment, m<=f''<=M and L=M-m>0. Put d=s-s0,
u=f'(s), u0=f'(s0), Delta=u-u0-m*d. Then
  f(s)>=f(s0)+u0*d+m*d^2/2+Delta^2/(2*L).                (13)
For d>=0, the derivative of f-m*t^2/2 is monotone and L-Lipschitz. Its
increment psi(t) satisfies
  psi(t)>=max(0,Delta-L*(d-t)), 0<=t<=d,
and 0<=Delta<=L*d. Integrating the right endpoint cone gives exactly
Delta^2/(2*L). For d<=0 apply the same argument to the reversed coordinate
t -> s0-t. Thus (13) has no hidden sign restriction on d. If L=0, the
derivative increment Delta is exactly zero and the constant-curvature
Taylor identity supplies the separate branch.

For any fixed real test number h_e, a square gives
  Delta^2/(2*L)>=h_e*Delta-L*h_e^2/2.
Consequently, including the constant-curvature branch,
  f(s)>=f0+u0*(d-h_e)+h_e*u
           +m*(d-h_e)^2/2-M*h_e^2/2.                   (14)
The test number h_e need not define another evaluation point in the cell.
Only s and s0 and the segment between them require the curvature bounds.

Take h_e=span_e(h) for a fixed rational nine-vector h. Summing (14)
retains all shared spans and collapses the 41 anchor derivatives into
the already stored aggregate gradient:
  F(g)>=F0+grad F(g0).(g-g0-h)+h.grad F(g)
       +sum_e a_e*m_e*span_e(g-g0-h)^2/2
       -sum_e a_e*M_e*span_e(h)^2/2.                    (15)
Thus arbitrary independent 41 values u0_e are not required. On an actual
KKT point one may delete h.grad F(g) only when h is supported on the
strictly free coordinates. The fixed pilot's nine coordinates are all
strictly free, checked against the original sep=123/128.

The old anchor gradient is stored as intervals [l_r,u_r]. Define its
midpoint q_r and error e_r. Replacing its term in (15) is paid by
  payment(h)=sum_r e_r*max(|cell_low_r-g0_r-h_r|,
                         |cell_high_r-g0_r-h_r|).       (16)
The payment MUST be recomputed for each h; the old payment(0) cannot be
silently reused. This is the only changed gradient-error payment in the
new row, and the existing F0 lower endpoint is retained.

Let H_m=sum_e a_e*m_e*v_e*v_e^T and
H_L=sum_e a_e*(M_e-m_e)*v_e*v_e^T. For a saved pseudo-moment vector with
mean dbar=<g>-g0, the gain over its old midpoint energy polynomial is
  -(q+H_m*dbar).h-h^T*H_L*h/2
             -[payment(h)-payment(0)].                 (17)
Ignoring the separately paid, extremely small interval uncertainty, if
H_L is positive definite this is maximized at
  h*= -H_L^(-1)*(q+H_m*dbar),
with ideal gain (q+H_m*dbar)^T*H_L^(-1)*(q+H_m*dbar)/2.
The inverse only selects a candidate. Every fixed rational h produces a
valid row independent of optimality. The helper uses exact elimination
with positive pivots and rounds h to denominator 2^20, then recomputes
(16) over the complete coordinate box.

At the original frozen first-remaining LP primal, the old energy polynomial
is approximately 0.0064428148536568. A fixed rational h selected as above
gives 0.012555875375836736 after the complete new error payment, strictly
above 11/1250. The exact decimal-primal violation is recorded in
cover-bregman-primal-check.json. This cuts that one saved moment vector;
it is not a proof of new-relaxation infeasibility.

The authorized Bregman pilot then made exactly two IPMs on the same cell:
original graph/KKT/RLT, original midpoint energy, and this first fixed-h
row; then one further h from that returned primal, retaining the first
row. The solves took about 1.64 and 1.50 seconds and both returned zero
energy slack. No residual-PSD cuts or reverse row were mixed in. No
Farkas extraction was triggered. The three energy packets are durable in
cover-bregman-energy-pilot.json. The branch stops at this fixed budget.

The Bregman formula and actual fixed-h semantic bridge still require

or these two zero-slack LPs supply no complete cover or new record.

7. Analytic elimination of all h, retaining only the old position moments
------------------------------------------------------------------------
This is a different construction from sampling further constant h. The
pointwise inequality (15) holds for every h, so at each actual g one may
choose the affine feedback
  h(g)=-B*(q+H_m*d), B=H_L^(-1), d=g-g0.
Assume H_L is positive definite and all h(g)'s coordinates are free. In
the fixed case all nine coordinates are strictly free. Thus h(g).grad F(g)
is zero at actual KKT, and its lifted polynomial elimination uses only
the existing first KKT and KKT*y equalities. The result is
  F(g)>=F0_lower+q.d+d^T*H_m*d/2
           +(q+H_m*d)^T*B*(q+H_m*d)/2 - payment_closed.  (18)
No new scalar anchor, derivative table, or 861-entry u-u block occurs.

The exact quadratic coefficients in d are
  K=H_m+H_m*B*H_m,
  linear=q+H_m*B*q,
  constant=F0_lower+q^T*B*q/2-payment_closed.
K is symmetric; this does not require commuting H_m and B. All inverse
identities are checked over Fraction, and the edgewise expansion of (15)
is replayed separately from this compact matrix formula.

The complete uncertainty payment is again essential. Put
  D=I+B*H_m, z=B*q, delta=center-g0.
Then d-h(g)=D*d+z. In the coordinate box g=center+radius*y, |y_i|<=1,
the valid exact upper bound is
  |(d-h(g))_r| <= |(D*delta+z)_r|
                        +sum_s |D_rs|*radius_s.
Multiplying these nine bounds by the original anchor-gradient interval
errors gives payment_closed. Neither fixed-h payment is reused.

At the last saved two-fixed-h LP primal, the closed row evaluates to
0.010778361690558121, exceeding 11/1250 by about 0.00197836. The exact
coefficient identity, inverse, error payment, and decimal-primal violation
are in cover-bregman-closed-primal-check.json. This is a strict cut of that
saved lifted vector, not a cell contradiction.

One final distinct pilot was authorized and completed: original base plus
original midpoint energy plus this one analytically eliminated row, with
no fixed-h sampled rows, residual PSD cuts, or reverse row. Its one IPM
took about 1.65 seconds and returned zero energy slack. No Farkas extraction

cover_bregman_coupling_replay.py independently rebuilds the reverse,

The closed row can carry more lifted information than all constant-h
tests: after averaging, the point-dependent choice keeps the quadratic

  trace(B*H_m*Cov(d)*H_m)/2>=0.
This observation is not a feasible-PSD-primal claim for the saved LPs.
