2. Global meromorphic amplitudes, on their valid domain
-----------------------------------------------------
Write y=pi*x. Evenness of sinc_rad gives
      KMT(x) = [sinc_rad(y-theta)+sinc_rad(y+theta)]/2.
For y != +/-theta, direct addition of these fractions gives
      KMT(x) = [y*cos(theta)*sin(y) - theta*sin(theta)*cos(y)]
               / (y^2-theta^2).
Consequently the normalized base term is
      [y*cos(theta)/(Z*(y^2-theta^2))]*sin(y) + B(x)*cos(y),
      B(x) = -theta*sin(theta)/(Z*(y^2-theta^2))
           = 1/(1-2*y^2).
In particular, the sign of B is fixed by this formula; replacing its
denominator by 2*y^2-1 without a minus sign would be an error.

For an integer m>=1 and x != +/-m, AMW.term_closed gives
      [sinc_rad(pi*(x-m))+sinc_rad(pi*(x+m))]/2
       = (-1)^m*x*sin(pi*x)/(pi*(x^2-m^2)).
The factor x arises from adding 1/(x-m) and 1/(x+m); neither a factor 1/2
nor an additional factor m belongs in this closed form.

Therefore, away from x=+/-theta/pi and x=+/-1,...,+/-24,
      k(x) = A(x)*sin(pi*x) + B(x)*cos(pi*x),
      A(x) = (1/Z) [ cos(theta)*pi*x/(pi^2*x^2-theta^2)
                     + sum(m=1..24) d_m*(-1)^m*x
                                      /(pi*(x^2-m^2)) ].       (1)
The whole sinc kernel is smooth at all the excluded points. The separate
amplitudes in (1) are not smooth there, so (1) cannot itself be used as a
uniform amplitude enclosure across an integer pole.

3. Isolate the resonant mode at an integer well
----------------------------------------------
Fix 1<=n<=24 and 0<r<1/2, with x in [n-r,n+r]. In particular x+n>0;
no other integer mode pole is in this interval. Also theta/pi<1/2, so the
base poles are absent. The existing r=21/100 convexity wells fit this domain.

Remove m=n from the sum in (1), and define
      A_n(x) = (1/Z) [ cos(theta)*pi*x/(pi^2*x^2-theta^2)
                       + sum(m=1..24,m!=n) d_m*(-1)^m*x
                                           /(pi*(x^2-m^2)) ],
      C_n(x) = d_n*x*S(x-n)/(Z*(x+n)).                         (2)
For delta=x-n !=0, sin(pi*x)=(-1)^n*sin(pi*delta). Thus the two parity
factors in the removed summand cancel, and that summand times sin(pi*x) is
exactly C_n(x). There is no remaining (-1)^n in C_n.

The regular identity is
      k(x) = C_n(x) + A_n(x)*sin(pi*x) + B(x)*cos(pi*x).        (3)
At x=n the sinc definition in (2) gives C_n(n)=d_n/(2*Z).
The finite-sinc formula gives the same value: the resonant pair is
[sinc_rad(0)+sinc_rad(2*pi*n)]/2=1/2, and all other integer-mode pairs
vanish. The base value is (-1)^n*B(n). Hence the exact center value is
      k(n) = d_n/(2*Z) + (-1)^n/(1-2*pi^2*n^2).               (4)
Equation (3) extends across x=n by continuity, or by checking this center
value separately. Establishing that extension as a new Lean lemma is still
future work; no total-field division convention should replace this step.

A_n and B are smooth rational functions of x with fixed transcendental
constants on this well. C_n is smooth, but contains S(x-n): it is not a pure
rational function of x. Its bounds and derivatives need an analytic payment.
For a well with integer center n>24, there is no resonant perturbation;
take C_n=0 and A_n=A. Negative wells can be treated by evenness of k and w.
Spans in the current separated positive-gap problem are >=123/128, so
the base poles +/-theta/pi are outside that problem's domain. A more general
domain crossing either base pole needs a separate regularization. For this
single-resonance parameterization, a cell crossing multiple integer poles
needs a subdivision or isolation of every resonant term; the following
all-mode option avoids the need to split at positive integer poles.

3a. Global smooth all-mode C, and the intermediate subset option
--------------------------------------------------------------
The same cancellation applies to an entire symmetric sinc pair, not just
its limit at the pole. For each m>=1 and EVERY x>0,
      (d_m/(2*Z))*[sinc_rad(pi*(x-m))+sinc_rad(pi*(x+m))]
       = d_m*x*S(x-m)/(Z*(x+m)).                            (2a)
For x!=m this follows from AMW.term_closed and
sin(pi*(x-m))=(-1)^m*sin(pi*x); at x=m both sides are d_m/(2*Z).
Since x+m>0, the right side has no positive integer pole. Therefore put
      C_all(x) = sum(m=1..24) d_m*x*S(x-m)/(Z*(x+m)),
      A0(x) = cos(theta)*pi*x/(Z*(pi^2*x^2-theta^2)).
Then, on x>=123/128,
      k(x) = C_all(x)+A0(x)*sin(pi*x)+B(x)*cos(pi*x).          (3a)
There is no positive integer pole in ANY of these three amplitudes; the
only base denominators vanish below the separated positive domain.
The first/second-harmonic and Hermitian constructions below apply to
(3a) as well as (3), using the corresponding amplitudes consistently.

More generally choose a fixed subset J of {1,...,24}, and put
      C_J(x) = sum(m in J) d_m*x*S(x-m)/(Z*(x+m)),
      A_J(x) = A0(x) + (1/Z)*sum(m not in J)
                               d_m*(-1)^m*x/(pi*(x^2-m^2)).
The identity k=C_J+A_J*sin(pi*x)+B*cos(pi*x) holds wherever the
unextracted denominators do not vanish. Choosing J to contain all integer
modes intersecting a cell gives regular amplitudes on that cell. J={n}
is (3); J={1,...,24} is (3a). This offers a tradeoff between the variation
of C_J and the regularity of A_J, without changing the kernel.

C_all is also the normalized Fourier transform of the perturbation:
      C_all(x)=(1/Z)*integral[-1/2,1/2]
                   [sum d_m*cos(2*pi*m*t)]*cos(2*pi*x*t) dt.
Its smoothness follows directly from this finite smooth integral. If
D=sum|d_m|, then elementary differentiation under this finite integral
and the triangle inequality give the paper-level global estimates
      |C_all^(r)(x)| <= (D/Z)*pi^r/(r+1),   r=0,1,2,... .     (3b)
Indeed the r-th cosine derivative is bounded by (2*pi*|t|)^r and
integral[-1/2,1/2]|t|^r dt=1/(2^r*(r+1)). Here
      D=78742791/10^9 <= 2/25.
The existing profile theorem already proves the coarser D<=2/25; neither
this new C_all definition nor (3b) has been formalized in Lean in this
audit. This identifies a straightforward sufficient source of coarse
uniform payments, not a numerically useful paid certificate.

C_all remains a sinc function sum, not a rational amplitude. It still
encodes oscillation and phase correlation in its x dependence. Extracting
all modes removes positive integer poles but does not justify freezing
C_all over a whole cell. Large errors from such freezing may offset the
benefit of using one globally smooth parameterization. The local and
all-mode versions should be compared with their actual payments.

4. Exact first and second harmonic expansion
-------------------------------------------
Use either A=A_n,C=C_n or A=A0,C=C_all, and B=B(x), phi=pi*x.
Squaring (3) or (3a),
      w(x) = C^2 + (A^2+B^2)/2
             +2*C*(A*sin(phi)+B*cos(phi))
             +(B^2-A^2)*cos(2*phi)/2 + A*B*sin(2*phi).        (5)
Equivalently, with complex amplitudes
      t = B-i*A,
      q0 = C^2+|t|^2/2,
      q1 = 2*C*t,
      q2 = t^2/2,
we have
      w(x) = q0 + Re(q1*exp(i*pi*x) + q2*exp(2*i*pi*x)).       (6)
In particular Im(q2)=-A*B. The minus sign in this imaginary part produces
the plus A*B*sin(2*phi) in (5). Omitting C_n would omit the first harmonic
and give an invalid identity at the integer wells of the new kernel.

These amplitudes are linked even before considering node phases:
      |q2|=(A^2+B^2)/2,  q0=C^2+|q2|,  q1^2=8*C^2*q2.
Treating q0,q1,q2 as unrelated interval variables can lose this structure.

5. From the actual 41 edges to two Hermitian forms
------------------------------------------------
StructuralGoal20261016/WindowGramExpansion.lean:16-29, namespace
StructuralGoal20261016.WindowGramTransfer, supplies the actual 41 positive
edges, their weights a_ij, and the already proved energy identity
      Fw24(g) = pressure(g) + sum(i<j,positive edges) a_ij*w(p_j-p_i),
where p_i=ptsN 10 g i. Use this actual pair ordering, or a checked mapping;
the DBM pair order and a numerical adjacency-first order are different.

For the moment evaluate all amplitudes at the SAME configuration p. Put
      u_i=exp(i*pi*p_i),       v_i=exp(2*i*pi*p_i)=u_i^2.
Then exp(i*pi*(p_j-p_i))=conj(u_i)*u_j, and similarly for v. Construct
two 10 by 10 Hermitian matrices with zero diagonal and, for i<j,
      (H1)_ij = a_ij*q1_ij/2,      (H2)_ij = a_ij*q2_ij/2,
      (H_l)_ji = conjugate((H_l)_ij).
Zero-weight pairs have zero entries. The exact identity is
      Fw24(g) = pressure(g) + sum a_ij*q0_ij
                           + u^* H1 u + v^* H2 v.            (7)
The factor 1/2 in each upper-triangular entry is necessary because the
Hermitian quadratic form counts both off-diagonal entries.

This is an exact re-expression, not an energy lower bound. In (7), the
matrices and constant term still depend on every real span p_j-p_i. A
numeric SDP using amplitudes only at a cell midpoint has not controlled
those matrices on the cell.

6. What a uniform rational certificate would need
------------------------------------------------
One valid future route is to choose rational reference A0_ij,B0_ij,C0_ij
for every edge and prove, on the entire cell,
      |A-A0|<=alpha, |B-B0|<=beta, |C-C0|<=gamma.              (8)
Let eta=alpha+beta, T=|A0|+|B0|, c=|C0|. The triangle inequality gives
the following sufficient payments for q0,q1,q2 relative to references
constructed by the same formulas as (6):
      eps0 = 2*c*gamma+gamma^2+T*eta+eta^2/2,
      eps1 = 2*(c*eta+gamma*T+gamma*eta),
      eps2 = T*eta+eta^2/2.                                 (9)
They bound |q0-q0_0|, |q1-q1_0|, |q2-q2_0|, respectively. These
conservative choices avoid an irrational norm in a rational certificate.
Since phases have modulus one and all actual a_ij are nonnegative,
      Fw24(g) >= pressure(g) + sum a_ij*q0_0,ij
                   +u^*H1_0 u+v^*H2_0 v
                   -sum a_ij*(eps0_ij+eps1_ij+eps2_ij).      (10)
This states a possible sufficient condition, not a condition already
proved for any candidate cell. Entrywise payments may erase the expected
gain; a verified matrix perturbation bound could be sharper.

For frozen rational Hermitian H_l, a rational PSD dual could certify
      H_l-diag(lambda_l) is positive semidefinite,
and therefore, for |u_i|=|v_i|=1,
      u^*H1*u+v^*H2*v >= sum_i(lambda1_i+lambda2_i).           (11)
A rational LDL/Gram factorization with exact residual sign conditions is
one possible checkable PSD certificate. No such dual, factorization, or
strict improved energy margin has been produced in this audit. Pressure
must also be retained or minimized over the actual cell geometry before
(10)-(11) could imply the desired floor.

Unproved work remaining for this route:
  (a) A Lean identity (3) or (3a), including its center and domain conditions.
  (b) Uniform real coefficient bounds (8), including the sinc in C_n,
      or a different rigorous bound on the matrix perturbation.
  (c) Exact rational PSD duals and a positive final margin after all
      coefficient and pressure payments.
  (d) A complete global certificate, with coverage over all relevant cells,
      before using the existing actual counting transfer to claim a record.
Existing actual k/w derivative and Taylor tools could support (a)-(b), but
they do not automatically prove these newly defined amplitudes' bounds.

7. Phase consistency and the relaxation issue
-------------------------------------------
The true variables satisfy v_i=u_i^2, not just |u_i|=|v_i|=1. Two separate
10 by 10 PSD duals, or a block-diagonal 20 by 20 Hermitian form with unit
diagonal constraints alone, do NOT enforce that relation. Independent
phase layers enlarge the feasible set: their lower bound remains valid
if all other payments are correct, but it can be much weaker.

Even a single node is a counterexample to automatic enforcement: u=1,v=-1
has unit moduli and a positive semidefinite rank-one Gram matrix for the
vector (u,v), yet v!=u^2. Thus adding arbitrary cross-layer Gram entries
and PSD alone also does not suffice. A moment/SOS lift retaining products,
or valid trigonometric consistency cuts, would require a precise theorem
and additional checkable constraints. Common node phases do retain cycle
relations within each layer; the missing issue is the relation BETWEEN
the layers and, after relaxation, their relation to real cell geometry.

An explicit single-edge weakness is k(phi)=1+cos(phi), with
      k(phi)^2=3/2+2*cos(phi)+(1/2)*cos(2*phi)>=0.
Independent rank-one phase layers u=(1,-1), v=(1,-1) assign cos(phi)=-1
and cos(2*phi)=-1, giving the relaxed value -1. This is a VALID lower
relaxation and shows a loss of square positivity; it is not a counterexample
to the actual ledger or to a strengthened phase method retaining geometry.

One natural necessary edge-level link uses z=X_ij, t=Y_ij and the Hermitian
Toeplitz moment matrix
      T(z,t) = [[1,       z,       t],
                [conj(z), 1,       z],
                [conj(t), conj(z), 1]].
If T is PSD, its minors imply |z|<=1 and
      |t-z^2| <= 1-|z|^2,
because det(T)=(1-|z|^2)^2-|t-z^2|^2. In particular |z|=1 forces
t=z^2. For coefficients of
      exp(i*phi)*k(phi)=(B+i*A)/2+C*exp(i*phi)
                                  +(B-i*A)*exp(2*i*phi)/2,
the corresponding T quadratic form is the moment value of k(phi)^2.
Thus this link preserves nonnegativity of every single-edge square with
frozen A,B,C. That does not establish jointly realizable node phases, the
actual span relation exp(i*pi*(p_j-p_i)), or any DBM/pressure relation.
It also does not supply the uniform payments when A,B,C vary with x.
No strengthened Toeplitz or moment certificate is generated by this note.

The matrix route has no algebraic sign or removable-pole obstruction on
the stated wells. Its quantitative value remains open. A practical first
test is one difficult existing cell: certify (8), solve the two-layer
relaxation, rationalize a dual, and subtract (9). If independent layers
already lose the available margin before payments, further moment
constraints or a different amplitude representation are needed before
attempting a large formalization.
